## Hook

The pass is snowed shut. The only way on is straight across a frozen mountain lake, and something is howling on the shore behind you.

## Setup

The tarn is 300 feet across. Crossing takes 5 rounds at a careful pace.
The ice is slippery: difficult terrain, or move at full speed with a Dexterity (Acrobatics) check against the DC, falling prone on a failure.
The pack appears on the shore in round 1 and follows at a distance.
It does not attack. It paces left and right, steering the party toward the middle.

## Twist

The pack is herding, and the middle of the lake is thin.
At the end of round 2 the ice breaks into floes, each about 15 feet across. From then on, at initiative count 0, every floe drifts 10 feet in a direction you choose, and gaps of open water open and close.
Jumping a gap of up to 10 feet: Strength (Athletics) against the DC. On a failure the creature is in the water and takes the hazard damage as cold at the start of each of its turns until it climbs out (an action, or half its movement with help).
A floe carrying more than two Medium creatures tips: everyone on it makes a Dexterity save against the DC or slides in.
The pack waits on solid ice at the far side for whoever arrives wet and alone. It attacks only creatures that are prone, in the water, or separated.
Reveal: a Wisdom (Survival) check against the DC in round 1 reads the pack's behaviour and spots the darker ice ahead.

## Terrain and tactics

Draw the floes on paper scraps and physically slide them each round.
The pack never steps on a floe.
If the party stays together and dry, the pack gives up at the shoreline.

## Ways out without a fight

Read the ice and take the long way around the rim: 8 rounds, no floes, and the pack shadows you but never commits.
Throw the pack your rations: Wisdom (Animal Handling) against the DC, and it loses interest.
Rope up. A roped creature that falls in is pulled out without an action.

## Ripple

Crossed cleanly: the party arrives early, and another table finds their rope line still staked across the lake.
Gear lost to the water: it washes up downstream for someone else to find.
Pack fed or spared: wolves are heard near the road, and herders at other tables are nervous.

## Reskin notes

Works as a log drive on a river, a field of drifting market barges, or a collapsing rope-bridge village.
Swap the pack for any patient hunter that will not fight fair.
