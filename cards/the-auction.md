## Hook

Lot nineteen is the thing you need, and you are not the only ones who came for it.

## Setup

A crowded sale room above a counting house. Thirty bidders, one auctioneer, two door wardens.
The party has a purse. Set it at 50 gp times the table's level.
Two rivals want lot nineteen: a collector with a deeper purse, and an agent bidding for someone unnamed.
Before the lot comes up, each character gets one scene to tilt the odds: learn a rival's limit, unsettle them, find a backer, examine the lot. Each is a check against the DC.
Every success lowers the price the party must beat by a fifth, or adds a fifth to their purse.

## Twist

Lot nineteen was stolen, and its owner has found it.
As the hammer rises, officers of the court walk in with a writ. The sale is frozen and the lot is evidence.
Everyone's position changes at once:
the auctioneer wants the lot gone and the room empty, and will sell privately in the next two minutes for half the last bid;
the collector will pay the party to carry it out the back;
the agent's employer is the rightful owner, and the agent now offers a reward instead of a bid;
the officers will take a sworn statement from anyone who helps, which is worth a favour later.
The party can deal with exactly one of them before the room is cleared.
Reveal: a Wisdom (Insight) check against the DC on the auctioneer, who keeps glancing at the door, or examining the lot and finding a filed-off owner's mark.

## Terrain and tactics

Nobody here fights. Weapons were checked at the door.
The rivals are polite, quick, and each wrong about one thing. Let a success tell the party what.
Give the party thirty real seconds to choose once the writ is read.

## Ways out without a fight

All of them. The choices are who to deal with, and whether to walk out with the lot, the reward, or clean hands.
Leaving through the counting house with the lot needs Dexterity (Stealth) or Charisma (Deception) against the DC.

## Ripple

Sided with the owner: a wealthy household owes the party, and other tables hear the name spoken well.
Bought it privately or carried it out: the party has the lot and a warrant, and the court's officers ask questions at other tables.
Sworn statement: the auction house closes for the season, and whatever else was due to be sold there is now loose in the city.

## Reskin notes

The lot is whatever your adventure needs: a map, a key, a caged animal, a debt.
Outside a city, run it as a horse fair or a harbour-side cargo sale, with the harbourmaster as the third party.
