## Hook

The river is rising and the ferry will cross once more before it is pulled up for a week. You have your seats. Three people on the bank do not, and all three start talking at once.

## Setup

The ferry holds the party and one more. The ferrywoman will not overload it and cannot be bribed past that.
It leaves in ten minutes. The next crossing is in seven days.
Three people want the last seat:
a midwife, called to a difficult birth on the far side tonight;
a clerk carrying a pardon for a prisoner who hangs across the river at dawn;
a drover with the season's wages for forty families, already two weeks late.
Each will plead their case. All three are telling the truth.

## Twist

There is no trick, and no fourth seat. Someone is left behind, and the party decides who.
Checks do not solve it. They only reveal what each choice costs:
a Wisdom (Insight) check against the DC shows that the midwife thinks the birth will go badly either way, but will not say so;
an Intelligence (Investigation) check against the DC on the pardon shows it is real, and that the prisoner did the thing he was condemned for;
a Wisdom (Insight) check against the DC shows the drover is late because he stopped to bury a friend, and is ashamed of it.
The party can give up seats of their own. Each character who stays loses the week, and whatever they were crossing for.
One thing can cross without a person: the pardon or the wages can be carried by a party member, if the clerk or the drover can be persuaded to hand them over (Charisma (Persuasion) against the DC, and a promise the party will be held to).
Reveal: none needed. Say plainly that all three are honest, once the players start hunting for a liar.

## Terrain and tactics

Let each claimant speak once, briefly, then let them interrupt each other.
The ferrywoman calls the minutes. At ten, she casts off with whoever is aboard.
Do not rescue the players with a second boat.

## Ways out without a fight

There is no fight. A drawn weapon ends with the ferry leaving empty.
The best outcomes come from carrying something across for someone, or from party members giving up their own seats.

## Ripple

Midwife crossed: a child is born across the river, and is named for whoever gave up the seat.
Pardon crossed: a guilty man walks free, and someone at another table knows what he did.
Wages crossed: forty families eat, and a village on the far bank is friendly to every table.
Whoever was left: they remember, and in a week they cross too.

## Reskin notes

A last coach before the snows, one cure for three patients, one signature a noble will give before leaving.
Choose the three claimants from people your tables already know to make it bite.
