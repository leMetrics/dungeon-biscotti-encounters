## Hook

A crypt, cold as a cellar. On the central tomb sits a lantern burning with a soft gold light, and the dead are standing up around it.

## Setup

The crypt is 40 by 30 feet with six alcoves and one stair up.
The lantern sits on the tomb in the middle. It is warm, and the light flickers like breathing.
The dead rise as the party enters and fight to keep them from the stair.
Each time one of the dead is destroyed, the lantern dims a little and the creature rises again at the start of the next round with half its hit points.

## Twist

The lantern is what raises them, and it is easy to break: AC 11, 5 hit points. Break it and every one of the dead falls at once.
The light inside is not oil. It is the breath of the villagers who fell sick above: one mote for each of them.
Break the lantern and they die tonight. Leave it and the dead keep rising.
The third way is slow: carry the lantern up the stair into daylight, 4 rounds of movement with the dead in pursuit. In the sun the motes fly home and the dead fall.
Whoever carries the lantern has one hand full and makes a Constitution save against the DC each round or takes the hazard damage as cold.
Reveal: an Intelligence (Arcana) or Wisdom (Medicine) check against the DC, or simply looking closely: the motes have faces. Tell the players plainly once anyone looks.

## Terrain and tactics

The dead ignore everyone except whoever is nearest the lantern.
The stair is 5 feet wide: one defender can hold it.
Alcoves give half cover.

## Ways out without a fight

Take the lantern and run for daylight. No attack rolls are needed if someone holds the stair.
Turn Undead and similar effects hold the dead for their duration, even though the lantern would raise them again.

## Ripple

Lantern carried out: the sickness in the village breaks overnight, at every table.
Lantern broken: the dead rest, but the village buries a dozen people, and someone asks who was down in the crypt.
Lantern left burning: the dead walk a little further each night. Another table will meet them.

## Reskin notes

The hostages can be the memories of a town, the voice of a choir, or the luck of a gambling house.
For a lighter tone the motes are the village's stolen sleep, and breaking the lantern means nobody ever dreams again.
