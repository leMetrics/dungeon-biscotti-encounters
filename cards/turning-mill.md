## Hook

The old watermill is grinding again, though the miller left years ago. Small shapes dart past its windows.

## Setup

The mill floor is a 30-foot circle with a 5-foot hub in the middle and four doorways: north, east, south, west.
The raiders have moved in and stacked their loot by the north door.
A brake lever sits on the hub. A sluice wheel is outside by the race.
The floor hums underfoot.

## Twist

The whole floor is the top of a great gear. At initiative count 0 each round it turns a quarter, clockwise.
Everything on the floor turns with it. The hub, the walls and the doorways do not.
A creature next to a wall when the floor turns makes a Dexterity save against the DC or is scraped for the hazard damage and falls prone.
The raiders know the rhythm. They step onto the hub or into a doorway just before each turn, and they shoot from doorways.
The brake lever stops the floor for 1 round (one action, Strength check against the DC). The sluice wheel outside stops it for good (two actions).
Reveal: tell the players the floor hums. Show them the first turn. Do not warn them.

## Terrain and tactics

Doorways give half cover and do not move.
Raiders shove targets toward the walls just before the turn.
The loot stack moves with the floor: it is at a different door each round.

## Ways out without a fight

Stop the sluice from outside first. Without the turning floor the raiders will bargain: they keep half the loot and leave.
Offer food. They moved in because they are hungry: Charisma (Persuasion) against the DC.

## Ripple

Mill stopped and cleared: the village can grind again, and flour is cheap next episode.
Raiders bargained with: they owe the party a favour and may turn up at another table.
Mill wrecked: the river floods the lower fields, and other tables find the ford gone.

## Reskin notes

Use any moving stage: a clock tower, a ferry on a cable, a theatre's revolving set.
The raiders can be any small, clever gang that has learned the machine.
